# Wheat cultivation in Maharashtra

Compiled from ICAR and Maharashtra State Department of Agriculture extension
guidance. Confirm against your own soil test and local KVK advice.

## Season and sowing

Wheat is a rabi crop. In Maharashtra the sowing window is **early November to
mid December**. Timely sowing matters more than almost anything else: yield
falls by roughly 1 percent for every day sown after 20 November, because the
grain-filling period then runs into the March heat.

Seed rate:

- Timely sown, irrigated: 40 to 50 kg per acre
- Late sown: 50 to 60 kg per acre (a higher plant stand compensates for less
  tillering time)
- Rain-fed: 30 to 35 kg per acre

Sow at 5 cm depth in rows 22.5 cm apart (18 cm for late sowing).

Treat seed before sowing with a fungicide against loose smut, then with
Azotobacter and PSB biofertiliser.

## Soil

Well-drained medium to heavy loam with pH 6.5 to 7.5. Black cotton soil grows
wheat well provided it drains. Prepare a fine, level seedbed — uneven land gives
uneven irrigation and uneven ripening.

## Fertiliser per acre

Apply 4 tonnes of farmyard manure per acre before ploughing.

For an irrigated timely-sown crop:

| Nutrient | Per acre | When |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N) | 48 kg | half basal, half at first irrigation (21 days) |
| Phosphorus (P) | 24 kg | all basal |
| Potassium (K) | 16 kg | all basal |

**In straight fertiliser terms, about 50 kg of urea per acre is applied at
sowing and another 50 kg at the first irrigation**, alongside 150 kg single
super phosphate and 27 kg muriate of potash at sowing.

For a rain-fed crop, cut nitrogen to about 20 kg per acre and apply it all at
sowing — there is no reliable irrigation to carry a top dressing into the plant.

Do not top-dress urea just before rain or heavy irrigation; it leaches away.

## Water

Wheat needs 4 to 6 irrigations. The critical stages, in order of importance:

1. **Crown root initiation, 20 to 25 days after sowing** — by far the most
   important. Missing this one irrigation can cost 30 percent of the yield.
2. Tillering, 40 to 45 days
3. Flowering, 60 to 65 days
4. Milk stage, 80 to 85 days
5. Dough stage, 100 to 105 days

If water is short and only two irrigations are possible, give them at crown root
initiation and at flowering.

## Main pests and diseases

**Aphids** — cluster on the ear head in warm weather. Ladybird beetles usually
control them; spray only if the population is very high.

**Termites** — a problem in light soils and where undecomposed organic matter
was ploughed in. Treat seed before sowing.

**Rusts (yellow, brown, black)** — orange to brown powdery pustules on leaves.
Yellow rust appears in cool weather. Grow resistant varieties; this is much more
effective than spraying.

**Loose smut** — the ear emerges as a black powdery mass. Seed-borne, so seed
treatment prevents it entirely. There is nothing to be done once the crop is
standing.

**Karnal bunt** — partly blackened grain with a fishy smell. Affects market
acceptance more than yield.

## Yield and economics

Irrigated: 16 to 22 quintals per acre. Rain-fed: 6 to 10 quintals per acre.

Typical cost of cultivation is 22,000 to 30,000 rupees per acre irrigated.

Wheat has a Minimum Support Price announced each year by the central government,
so unlike vegetables there is a price floor. Selling at a government procurement
centre requires land records (7/12 extract), an Aadhaar card and a bank account
in the farmer's own name.

## After harvest

Harvest when the grain is hard and contains about 20 percent moisture, then dry
to 12 percent before storage. Grain stored above 12 percent moisture develops
mould and insects.

Store in a clean, dry, well-ventilated room. Neem leaves layered in the storage
bin discourage insects without chemicals.
