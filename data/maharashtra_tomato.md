# Tomato cultivation in Maharashtra

Compiled from ICAR and Maharashtra State Department of Agriculture extension
guidance. Figures are typical recommendations — always confirm against your own
soil test and local KVK advice before applying.

## Season and sowing

Three seasons are grown in Maharashtra:

- **Kharif** — nursery sowing June to July, transplant July to August.
- **Rabi** — nursery sowing September to October, transplant October to November.
  This is the main season for quality and yield.
- **Summer** — nursery December to January, transplant January to February.
  Needs assured irrigation.

Seed rate is 400 to 500 grams per hectare (about 160 to 200 grams per acre) for
hybrids, and 250 to 300 grams per hectare for open-pollinated varieties.
Transplant seedlings at 25 to 30 days old, when they have 4 to 5 true leaves.

Spacing: 60 x 45 cm for determinate varieties, 90 x 60 cm for indeterminate
hybrids grown on stakes.

## Soil

Well-drained loam or sandy loam with pH 6.0 to 7.0. Tomato does not tolerate
waterlogging — even a few hours of standing water causes wilt and root rot. On
heavy black cotton soil, plant on raised beds 15 to 20 cm high.

## Fertiliser per acre

Apply 8 to 10 tonnes of well-rotted farmyard manure per acre before the last
ploughing.

| Nutrient | Total per acre | Basal | 30 days after transplant | 60 days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N) | 40 kg | 1/3 | 1/3 | 1/3 |
| Phosphorus (P) | 20 kg | all | — | — |
| Potassium (K) | 20 kg | half | half | — |

As straight fertilisers this is roughly 87 kg urea, 125 kg single super
phosphate and 33 kg muriate of potash per acre across the season.

Calcium deficiency causes blossom-end rot — a black sunken patch at the bottom
of the fruit. It is usually caused by irregular watering rather than a lack of
calcium in the soil, so keep irrigation even.

## Water

Irrigate immediately after transplanting, then every 6 to 8 days in winter and
every 4 to 5 days in summer. Drip irrigation saves about 40 percent of water and
keeps the leaves dry, which cuts fungal disease sharply. Critical stages are
flowering and fruit development — water stress at flowering causes flower drop
and directly reduces yield.

## Main pests

**Fruit borer (Helicoverpa armigera)** — the most damaging pest. Bores a neat
round hole into the fruit. Install pheromone traps at 5 per acre to monitor.
Spray only when damage crosses 5 percent of fruits. Neem-based sprays work on
early stages. Rotate chemical groups so resistance does not build up.

**Whitefly** — sucks sap and transmits leaf curl virus. Yellow sticky traps at
10 per acre. Control matters more for the virus than for the direct damage.

**Leaf miner** — makes winding white tunnels in the leaf. Usually cosmetic;
remove badly affected leaves and do not spray unless severe.

## Main diseases

**Early blight** — brown spots with concentric rings on older leaves. Favoured
by warm humid weather. Remove affected leaves, avoid overhead irrigation.

**Late blight** — water-soaked patches spreading fast in cool humid weather
below 20 degrees with high humidity. Can destroy a crop in days. Act at the
first sign.

**Tomato leaf curl virus** — leaves curl upward and the plant stunts. There is
no cure once infected; pull out and destroy affected plants and control the
whitefly that spreads it. Prefer tolerant varieties.

**Bacterial wilt** — the plant wilts suddenly while still green. Common in black
soils. Rotate away from tomato, brinjal, chilli and potato for 3 years.

## Yield and economics

Open-pollinated varieties give 8 to 12 tonnes per acre; hybrids give 15 to 25
tonnes per acre under good management.

Typical cost of cultivation is 55,000 to 80,000 rupees per acre for a staked
hybrid crop, of which staking and labour are the largest items.

Tomato prices are extremely volatile — the same crop can fetch 400 rupees or
4,000 rupees per quintal within one season. Staggered planting across three or
four dates spreads that risk far better than planting the whole field at once.

## After harvest

Harvest at the mature green or breaker stage for distant markets, and at the
pink stage for local sale. Grade by size and colour. Do not heap more than 4 or
5 crates deep — the fruit at the bottom crushes. Store at 12 to 15 degrees; do
not refrigerate below 10 degrees, which causes chilling injury and a mealy
texture.
