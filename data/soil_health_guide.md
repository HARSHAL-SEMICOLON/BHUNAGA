# Understanding your soil

## Reading a Soil Health Card

The card reports 12 parameters. These are the ones that change what you do.

### pH — how acidic or alkaline

| Reading | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Below 5.5 | Strongly acidic | Apply lime. Until you do, phosphorus stays locked and the crop cannot use it |
| 5.5 to 6.5 | Slightly acidic | Fine for most crops; ideal for potato |
| 6.5 to 7.5 | Neutral | Ideal for nearly everything |
| 7.5 to 8.5 | Slightly alkaline | Common in black soils. Add organic matter |
| Above 8.5 | Strongly alkaline / sodic | Gypsum plus organic matter. Expect iron and zinc deficiency |

pH is not a nutrient, but it controls whether the nutrients present can be taken
up at all. Fertilising a field with badly wrong pH is pouring money into soil
that cannot deliver it.

### Organic carbon — the single most important number

| Reading | Rating |
|---|---|
| Below 0.5 percent | Low |
| 0.5 to 0.75 percent | Medium |
| Above 0.75 percent | High |

Most Maharashtra soils test low. This is the number worth attacking first,
because raising it makes every other input work better. Nothing else on the card
gives a comparable return.

Raise it with: farmyard manure, vermicompost, green manure (dhaincha or sunhemp
ploughed in before flowering), leaving crop residue on the field instead of
burning it, and keeping a legume in the rotation.

It moves slowly. Expect to see a change over 3 to 4 seasons, not one.

### Nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium

Reported as available N, P and K in kg per hectare, with a Low / Medium / High
rating. Follow the card's own crop-wise recommendation, which is calculated for
your reading.

Two habits waste the most money:

- **Applying urea because it is cheap and visible.** Excess nitrogen gives lush
  leaves, delayed maturity, and more pest attack, not more grain.
- **Skipping potassium.** It does not produce a visible flush of growth, so it
  gets dropped — but it is what fills grain and tuber and gives keeping quality.

### Micronutrients

Zinc, iron, manganese, copper, boron and sulphur. Deficiencies are common and
cheap to fix.

- **Zinc** — the commonest deficiency in Indian soils. Stunted plants, small
  leaves. 10 kg zinc sulphate per acre, once every 2 or 3 years
- **Iron** — yellowing between the veins on young leaves, worst in alkaline soil.
  Foliar ferrous sulphate spray works better than soil application
- **Sulphur** — matters greatly for oilseeds and pulses. Routinely forgotten
- **Boron** — hollow stem, cracked fruit. Apply carefully; the gap between enough
  and too much is narrow

## Taking a soil sample properly

A bad sample makes the whole card meaningless.

1. Sample after harvest and before the next fertiliser application
2. Take from 10 to 15 spots spread across the field, walking a zigzag
3. At each spot clear the surface trash, then dig a V to 15 cm and take a 2 cm
   slice from the side of the V
4. Mix all of it in a clean bucket
5. Quarter the heap, discard opposite quarters, repeat until about 500 grams is
   left
6. Air dry in shade. Do not dry in sun or in an oven
7. Label with name, survey number, area and the crop you plan to grow

Do not sample near a bund, a tree, a manure heap, or an old fertiliser band —
those spots are not representative of the field.

Sample separately for parts of the field that clearly differ in colour, slope or
past cropping. Averaging a good patch with a bad one describes neither.

Cards are free under the Soil Health Card Scheme through your Taluka Agriculture
Office or a Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

## Soil types in Maharashtra

**Black cotton soil (regur)** — most of Vidarbha and Marathwada. High clay, holds
water well, cracks when dry. Good for cotton, soybean, tur, gram. Works badly
when wet — ploughing it wet sets it hard. Needs drainage.

**Red soil** — parts of Konkan and the east. Lower fertility, drains fast,
usually acidic. Responds strongly to organic matter.

**Laterite** — Konkan. Acidic, low in nutrients, drains very fast. Suits cashew,
mango and coconut.

**Alluvial** — river valleys. The most fertile, good for almost anything.

**Sandy loam** — parts of western Maharashtra. Drains fast, low water holding.
Irrigate little and often and split fertiliser doses, or both leach away.

## Things that damage soil, slowly and invisibly

- **Burning crop residue.** Destroys organic matter and kills the soil life. The
  fertility lost each year is worth more than the labour saved
- **Growing the same crop every season.** Builds up its specific pests and
  diseases and depletes the same nutrients
- **Working the soil when it is wet.** Compacts it into a hard layer that roots
  cannot cross
- **Over-irrigating on flat ground.** Brings salts to the surface. Once a field
  turns saline, recovery takes years
