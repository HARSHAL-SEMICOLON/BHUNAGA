# Tur (pigeon pea) and Gram (chickpea) in Maharashtra

Compiled from ICAR and Maharashtra State Department of Agriculture extension
guidance. Confirm against your own soil test and local KVK advice.

Both are pulses, and both fix their own nitrogen from the air through root
nodules. This has a practical consequence farmers often miss: **they need far
less nitrogen fertiliser than a cereal, and applying a lot of urea actually
suppresses nodulation** and wastes money twice over.

---

## Tur / Arhar / Pigeon pea

### Season and sowing

A kharif crop. Sow with the onset of the monsoon, **mid June to mid July**.
Late sowing after July shortens the vegetative period and cuts yield sharply.

Seed rate: 6 to 8 kg per acre for medium-duration varieties, 4 to 5 kg for long
duration. Spacing 90 x 20 cm for medium duration, 120 x 30 cm for long duration.

Treat the seed with Rhizobium culture before sowing. On land that has not grown
tur for several years this can add 15 percent to the yield on its own, because
the right bacteria are not present in the soil.

### Soil

Deep, well-drained black soil. pH 6.5 to 7.5. Tur is very sensitive to
waterlogging — 2 or 3 days of standing water will kill the plant outright. On
flat black soil, sow on broad beds with furrows.

### Fertiliser per acre

| Nutrient | Per acre | When |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N) | 10 kg | all at sowing — a starter dose only |
| Phosphorus (P) | 20 kg | all at sowing |
| Sulphur | 8 kg | all at sowing |

Sulphur matters for pulses and is routinely forgotten. It goes directly into
protein formation, and black soils in Maharashtra are often short of it.

### Main pests

**Pod borer (Helicoverpa)** — the main enemy. Larvae bore into the pod and eat
the developing grain. Flowering to pod formation is the vulnerable window.
Pheromone traps at 5 per acre to monitor. Bird perches at 10 per acre are cheap
and genuinely effective — birds eat a large share of the larvae.

**Pod fly** — maggots feed inside the pod; the damage is invisible from outside
until you open it.

**Wilt (Fusarium)** — plants dry up from flowering onwards, often in patches.
Soil-borne and persistent. Rotate crops and grow resistant varieties; there is
no spray that will fix it.

### Yield

6 to 10 quintals per acre. Tur has a Minimum Support Price.

## Gram / Chana / Chickpea

### Season and sowing

A rabi crop. Sow **mid October to mid November** on residual soil moisture after
the monsoon.

Seed rate: 30 to 35 kg per acre for desi types, 45 to 50 kg for the larger
kabuli types. Spacing 30 x 10 cm.

Treat seed with Rhizobium and PSB culture.

### Soil

Well-drained medium to heavy black soil. pH 6.0 to 7.5. Gram is grown largely
rain-fed on stored moisture, which is why a good final ploughing to conserve
that moisture matters so much.

### Fertiliser per acre

| Nutrient | Per acre |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N) | 8 kg, starter only |
| Phosphorus (P) | 16 kg |
| Sulphur | 8 kg |

### Water

Usually one or two irrigations are enough, at branching (30 days) and at pod
formation (60 to 70 days). Do not over-irrigate — excess water causes rank
vegetative growth with plenty of leaf and very few pods.

### Main pests and diseases

**Pod borer** — again the main pest, same management as in tur.

**Wilt** — the most important disease. Plants die in patches from the seedling
stage onwards. Long rotation and resistant varieties are the answer.

**Botrytis grey mould** — in cool humid weather with a dense canopy. Wider
spacing and avoiding excess nitrogen both reduce it.

### Yield

6 to 9 quintals per acre rain-fed, 10 to 14 quintals irrigated. Gram has a
Minimum Support Price.

## Why pulses are worth growing even when the price is poor

A pulse crop leaves 20 to 40 kg of nitrogen per hectare in the soil for the next
crop, and its deep roots break compacted layers. A wheat crop following a pulse
regularly yields better than wheat following wheat, on the same land with the
same fertiliser. That benefit does not appear in the pulse crop's own accounts,
but it is real, and it is the strongest argument for keeping a pulse in the
rotation.
