# Potato cultivation in Maharashtra

Compiled from ICAR and Maharashtra State Department of Agriculture extension
guidance. Confirm against your own soil test and local KVK advice.

## Season and planting

Potato is a rabi crop in Maharashtra. Plant from **mid October to mid November**,
once night temperatures fall below about 20 degrees. Tubers do not form well
above 30 degrees, which is why late planting fails.

Seed rate is 800 kg to 1 tonne per acre — the single largest cost in the crop.
Use certified seed tubers of 25 to 40 grams, or cut pieces each carrying at
least two healthy eyes. Cut tubers 2 to 3 days before planting and let the cut
surface dry, otherwise they rot in the soil.

Spacing: 60 cm between ridges, 20 cm between plants, planted 5 to 6 cm deep on
the ridge.

## Soil

Loose, well-drained sandy loam is ideal. pH 5.5 to 6.5 — potato is one of the
few crops that prefers slightly acidic soil. Heavy clay produces misshapen
tubers and makes harvesting difficult.

Earthing up is essential. Do it twice, at 25 and 45 days. Tubers exposed to
light turn green and develop solanine, which is bitter and unfit to eat or sell.

## Fertiliser per acre

Apply 8 to 10 tonnes of farmyard manure per acre.

| Nutrient | Per acre | When |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N) | 48 kg | half at planting, half at first earthing up |
| Phosphorus (P) | 32 kg | all at planting |
| Potassium (K) | 40 kg | all at planting |

Potato is a heavy potassium feeder — potassium is what fills the tuber and gives
it keeping quality. Skimping on it is a common and expensive mistake.

## Water

Irrigate lightly right after planting, then every 7 to 10 days. Keep the soil
evenly moist but never waterlogged. The critical period is **tuber initiation
and bulking, 30 to 60 days after planting**.

Irregular watering — dry, then heavy irrigation — causes cracked and knobbly
tubers that fetch a much lower price.

Stop irrigating 10 to 15 days before harvest so the skin sets firm.

## Main pests and diseases

**Late blight** — the most serious potato disease. Water-soaked dark patches on
leaves with a white fungal growth underneath in humid weather, spreading to a
whole field within a week. Cool, cloudy, humid weather is the danger signal.
Watch the forecast and act preventively rather than after symptoms appear.

**Early blight** — brown spots with concentric rings, mainly on older leaves.
Less destructive than late blight.

**Potato tuber moth** — larvae tunnel into tubers, in the field and in storage.
Keep tubers well covered with soil by earthing up properly, and never leave
harvested tubers exposed in the field overnight.

**Aphids** — spread viruses that degenerate seed stock over generations. This is
the reason to buy certified seed rather than replanting your own year after year.

## Yield and economics

8 to 12 tonnes per acre under good management.

Typical cost of cultivation is 50,000 to 70,000 rupees per acre, with seed alone
accounting for around half of it.

## After harvest

Cure the harvested tubers in shade for 10 to 15 days so the skin hardens. Grade
by size. Discard any cut, green or diseased tubers before storing — one rotting
tuber spreads to the whole heap.

Cold storage is at 2 to 4 degrees for seed potato and 10 to 12 degrees for table
potato. Storing table potato too cold turns starch to sugar, which makes it
sweet and turns it dark on frying.

Without cold storage, keep in a dark, cool, ventilated room in a layer no more
than 60 cm deep, and check the heap every couple of weeks.
